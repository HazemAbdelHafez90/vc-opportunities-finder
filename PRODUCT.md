# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Fairpicture operations and business development team. They open Tender Radar once or a few times a week to scan for new humanitarian media and visual storytelling procurement opportunities. Their primary job: quickly identify which tenders are worth pursuing, ranked by fit for documentary, photography, and field production work. They are busy, discerning, and familiar with the tool; they want signal with minimum friction.

## Product Purpose

Tender Radar is an internal procurement intelligence tool for Fairpicture. It continuously scans ReliefWeb, UNDP Procurement, UNGM, ICIMOD, Welthungerhilfe, and other humanitarian sources, ranks results by fit for visual/documentary work, and presents them in a fast, filterable desk. Success means the team spends less time searching and more time bidding on the right opportunities.

## Brand Personality

Precise, operational, quietly confident. Three words: sharp, purposeful, trusted.

The tool exists inside a creative documentary agency; it should feel like a well-designed newsroom tool, not generic enterprise software. Dark environment, high information density, but never hostile — every element should feel intentional.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboards: Notion, Airtable — clean-but-bland, card-heavy, neutral grays
- Enterprise procurement tools: SAP, Oracle — dense, corporate, visually hostile
- NGO/UN procurement portals (UNGM, UNDP UI) — the very sources it scrapes; bureaucratic, dated, no craft
- Startup analytics dashboards — hero metrics, gradient charts, blue-heavy SaaS palette

## Design Principles

1. **Signal over chrome.** Every visual element should help the team find the right tender faster. Decoration that doesn't serve that goal gets cut.
2. **Quiet confidence.** The tool knows what it is. No explanatory filler, no onboarding theater. Assume the user knows the domain.
3. **Density with breathing room.** High information density is appropriate; claustrophobia is not. Spacing is a tool, not decoration.
4. **Red means action.** The accent (Fairpicture red) is reserved for interactive affordances and priority signals. Never decorative.
5. **Dark by default.** The team reviews tenders in a dim office environment, often alongside other screens. Dark theme reduces fatigue and improves focus during extended sessions.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Best effort, not formally audited. Aim for WCAG AA contrast on all body text, clear focus indicators for keyboard users, and meaningful ARIA labels on interactive elements. The user base is small and internal; no specific accommodation requirements identified.
